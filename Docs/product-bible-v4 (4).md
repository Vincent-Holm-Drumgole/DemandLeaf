# PART 1: PRODUCT VISION & POSITIONING

## 1.1 Mission Statement

[Product Name] is an AI-native content intelligence platform that helps companies create SEO and AEO-optimized blog content that sounds authentically human, ranks in both traditional search and AI answer engines, and is indistinguishable from expert human writing.

## 1.2 The Problem (First Principles)

A company needs blog content to grow organically. The current options are:

- **Hire writers**: Expensive ($200-500 per post for quality), slow (days to weeks per post), inconsistent quality, hard to scale, writers rarely understand SEO deeply
- **Use generic AI tools**: Detectable as AI, generic voice, no strategic intelligence, no SEO depth, content that reads like everyone else's content
- **Do nothing**: Invisible in search, invisible in AI answers, competitors take all the organic traffic

The deeper problem: even companies that DO publish blogs often publish content that doesn't rank because it lacks strategy, targets the wrong keywords, doesn't match search intent, or isn't differentiated enough from what's already ranking.

The deepest problem: the quality of AI-generated content is determined by the quality of the inputs. Most AI writing tools accept a keyword and output generic text. They never ask: what does this company uniquely know? What has their team experienced firsthand? What data do they have that nobody else does? Without this, all AI content converges to the same bland middle.

## 1.3 Core Value Proposition

[Product Name] combines deep brand intelligence, research-backed SEO/AEO optimization, strategic keyword intelligence, and a proprietary AI writing system that produces content indistinguishable from skilled human writers. It doesn't just write blogs. It runs your entire content operation from strategy to publishing to performance optimization, and over time, it does so autonomously.

## 1.4 The 10-Minute "Holy Shit" Moment

A new user pastes their website URL. The platform crawls it, analyzes the voice, maps existing content, and within minutes generates a complete blog post draft that sounds like the company wrote it, with full SEO optimization, in a content archetype appropriate for their business. No configuration required. No knowledge base upload. No strategy setup. Just magic.

Then all the depth (strategy, AEO, keyword intelligence, WordPress publishing, agentic operations) becomes the reason they stay and pay.

**Zero-configuration must be good enough. Full configuration must be great.**

## 1.5 Target Users

- **Solo marketers and founders** wearing multiple hats who want content to just happen (highest volume, fastest time-to-value requirement)
- **Marketing teams at SMBs and mid-market companies** who lack dedicated content teams but need consistent blog output
- **Marketing agencies** managing content for multiple clients (highest LTV, need multi-tenant and white-label)
- **Enterprise marketing ops teams** looking to scale content without scaling headcount
- **Content leads and writers** who want AI to produce first drafts they can polish, not replace them

## 1.6 Two Usage Modes

**Mode 1: AI-Generated**
The platform writes the complete blog. User reviews, provides feedback, approves. Best for: solo marketers, small teams, high-volume needs.

**Mode 2: AI-Assisted**
The platform generates a structured first draft with SEO optimization baked in. Human writers polish it, add their voice, inject personal experience. The system learns from the edits over time to get closer to the writer's quality. Best for: agencies, content teams with writers, quality-obsessed brands.

## 1.7 Non-Negotiable Pillars

1. **Writing quality indistinguishable from a skilled human writer**
2. **Every blog optimized against best-in-class SEO and AEO standards**
3. **Brand voice fidelity that makes content sound like it came from the company**
4. **Strategic intelligence that drives what to write, not just how to write it**

6. **Time to first value under 10 minutes with zero configuration**
7. **Maximum profitability through efficient architecture and smart model routing**

## 1.8 The Quality Promise (Not "Undetectable")

Do not promise "undetectable AI writing." Detection landscapes shift constantly. If a customer's content gets flagged, you've broken trust. Instead promise: **"Human-quality writing built on best practices for natural, authentic content."** The anti-detection system is a feature that supports the quality promise. The quality itself is the brand promise.

## 1.9 Defensibility: The Compounding Data Flywheel

Every blog published generates performance data. Every piece of user feedback improves the voice model. Every strategy outcome teaches the system which keywords, structures, and approaches actually work for specific industries and company types. Over time, the platform develops proprietary intelligence about what content actually ranks, for which types of companies, in which niches. No competitor who starts later can replicate this accumulated learning. The flywheel must start compounding from day one.

# PART 3: CONTENT ARCHETYPES

## 3.1 Why Archetypes Matter

"A blog" is not one thing. Different content types serve different purposes, attract different engagement, require different writing structures, and have different success metrics. The system must understand and handle each archetype differently, from the brief template to the writing structure to the quality gates to how performance is measured.

## 3.2 The Eight Content Archetypes

### Archetype 1: Thought Leadership / Hot Take
**Purpose:** Establish authority, generate discussion, earn links and shares
**Funnel stage:** Top of funnel (awareness + brand building)
**SEO role:** Attracts backlinks, builds E-E-A-T, creates branded search demand
**AEO role:** Gets cited when AI tools reference industry perspectives
**Writing approach:** Confident, opinionated, personal. Lead with the take, not the setup.
**Typical length:** 800-1,500 words
**Success metrics:** Backlinks earned, social shares, branded search lift, time on page
**Unique requirement:** Requires a genuine opinion from the company's thought leadership positions. Cannot be faked.
**Brief additions:** What is the contrarian position? What evidence supports it? How do we address disagreement?

### Archetype 2: How-To / Tutorial
**Purpose:** Solve a specific problem, capture search traffic, demonstrate expertise
**Funnel stage:** Middle of funnel (consideration)
**SEO role:** Captures long-tail traffic, earns featured snippets, high dwell time
**AEO role:** Step-by-step structure is ideal for AI citation
**Writing approach:** Clear, patient, detailed. Less personality, more utility.
**Typical length:** 1,500-3,000 words
**Success metrics:** Organic traffic, featured snippet capture, time on page, scroll depth
**Unique requirement:** Specific process details, real tips from actual experience.
**Brief additions:** What are the exact steps? Common mistakes? What does success look like?

### Archetype 3: Definitive Guide / Pillar Content
**Purpose:** Comprehensive resource, anchor for topic clusters
**Funnel stage:** Spans awareness through consideration
**SEO role:** Builds topical authority, targets competitive head terms
**AEO role:** Multiple extractable sections for different AI queries
**Writing approach:** Authoritative, well-researched, table of contents required.
**Typical length:** 3,000-5,000+ words
**Success metrics:** Head term rankings, internal link equity, backlinks, traffic over time
**Unique requirement:** Comprehensive expertise. Reference company experience and frameworks.
**Brief additions:** What subtopics are essential? What do top guides miss? What's our unique angle?

### Archetype 4: Comparison / Versus
**Purpose:** Capture commercial intent searches, influence buying decisions
**Funnel stage:** Bottom of funnel (decision)
**SEO role:** Captures high-intent keywords ("X vs Y", "best X for Y")
**AEO role:** Comparison data frequently cited for "which is better" queries
**Writing approach:** Balanced with clear perspective. Data-driven. Tables.
**Typical length:** 1,500-2,500 words
**Success metrics:** Organic traffic, conversion rate, featured snippet capture
**Unique requirement:** Real experience with products being compared.
**Brief additions:** Comparison criteria? Who should choose A vs B? Our recommendation?

### Archetype 5: Data Study / Original Research
**Purpose:** Earn backlinks, establish authority, generate PR, fuel AEO citations
**Funnel stage:** Top of funnel (awareness + link building)
**SEO role:** Link magnet, citation magnet, massive E-E-A-T builder
**AEO role:** Statistics get cited heavily by AI tools. Highest AEO potential.
**Writing approach:** Analytical, precise, clear methodology, key findings highlighted.
**Typical length:** 2,000-4,000 words
**Success metrics:** Backlinks, press mentions, AI citations, social shares
**Unique requirement:** Requires actual proprietary data. Cannot be fabricated.
**Brief additions:** What data do we have? What question does it answer? Key findings?

### Archetype 6: Listicle / Resource Roundup
**Purpose:** Capture search traffic, provide quick value, earn social shares
**Funnel stage:** Top to middle of funnel
**SEO role:** Captures "best X" and "top X" searches, featured snippets
**AEO role:** List items easily extractable for recommendation queries
**Writing approach:** Concise per item with enough depth to be useful.
**Typical length:** 1,500-2,500 words
**Success metrics:** Organic traffic, social shares, CTR
**Unique requirement:** Real experience with items listed.
**Brief additions:** What items and why? Our firsthand experience? Organizing principle?

### Archetype 7: Case Study / Customer Story
**Purpose:** Social proof, demonstrate results, support sales
**Funnel stage:** Bottom of funnel (decision)
**SEO role:** Captures long-tail problem-specific searches
**AEO role:** AI tools cite specific results when recommending solutions
**Writing approach:** Narrative: situation, challenge, solution, result.
**Typical length:** 1,000-2,000 words
**Success metrics:** Conversion influence, organic traffic
**Unique requirement:** Actual customer data and story from input system.
**Brief additions:** Customer profile? Specific problem? Result with numbers? Quote?

### Archetype 8: News / Trend Commentary
**Purpose:** Demonstrate industry awareness, capture timely traffic
**Funnel stage:** Top of funnel (awareness)
**SEO role:** Captures trending terms, signals freshness
**AEO role:** Freshness matters: half of AI citations from content under 11 months old
**Writing approach:** Fast, opinionated, ties news to reader's situation.
**Typical length:** 600-1,200 words
**Success metrics:** Speed to publish, timely traffic, social engagement
**Unique requirement:** Company's perspective on the news.
**Brief additions:** What is the news? Our perspective? What should our audience do?

## 3.3 Archetype Selection Logic

Recommend based on: keyword search intent, funnel stage gaps, content calendar balance, available inputs (data = data study, opinions = thought leadership, customer stories = case study), and competitive landscape.

# PART 4: THE NARRATIVE ENGINE

## 4.1 The Problem with SEO-Only Writing

Most SEO content is structurally correct but narratively dead. Google measures engagement. If users bounce, the content drops. Technical optimization without narrative quality is a losing strategy.

## 4.2 The Three Narrative Elements

### The Hook (First 2-3 Sentences)
Do one of these: open a curiosity gap, make a surprising claim, tell a micro-story, challenge an assumption, state a specific concrete fact.

Never: "In today's digital landscape...", a generic definition, a long preamble.

### Momentum Maintenance (Every Section Transition)
End sections with: a question, a "but", a tease, or tension.

Never: "Now let's move on to...", "In summary, X. Moving forward, let's look at Y."

### The Payoff (Closing)
Reader must get: a framework to apply, a clear action, a confident decision, or a statistic to share.

Never: "In conclusion, [restate everything]", generic CTA disconnected from content.

## 4.3 Implementation

For each brief, generate: 3 hook options (user picks one), section transition suggestions, and a payoff recommendation based on archetype.

# PART 6: SEO ENGINE

## 6.1 The SEO Landscape in 2026

Top ranking factors: quality original content, backlink quality, E-E-A-T signals, technical SEO, topical authority, user engagement, search intent matching, LSI keywords, content freshness.

Critical stats: 97% of AI Overview citations come from top-20 organic results. Only 47-54% of sites meet Core Web Vitals. Google rolled out 12 algorithm updates in 2024-2025. Brands in AI Overviews see +35% organic clicks.

## 6.2 The Intent-First Writing Architecture

Start with intent, not keywords. Structure: Hook (1-2 sentences) > Answer (first 100-200 words, satisfy core intent) > Depth (body with unique insights) > Action (clear next step).

## 6.3 SEO Optimization Checklist

**Focus Keyphrase:** Set, in SEO title, in first 10% of content, in meta description, in URL, in at least one H2/H3, 0.5-1.5% density, no cannibalization, LSI keywords present.

**Title & Meta:** SEO title 50-60 chars with power word/number, unique. Meta description 120-155 chars with hook and CTA, unique.

**Content Structure:** Length matches/exceeds competitors, H1 once, H2/H3 logical, short paragraphs, images with alt text, 2-3+ internal links, 1-2 external links, TOC for 1,500+ word posts.

**Technical:** Clean URL (3-5 words, keyphrase, hyphens, lowercase), canonical set, schema markup, OG/Twitter cards, compressed images.

**Readability:** Flesch score appropriate for audience, sentence length variation, 80%+ active voice, visual breaks every 300 words.

**Unique Value:** 1+ proprietary insight from knowledge base, angle not in top 5 results, demonstrates firsthand experience.

**Featured Snippet Targeting:** Paragraph snippet (40-60 word answer below heading), list snippet (proper HTML), table snippet (comparison data).

**People Also Ask:** Top 3-5 PAA questions as subsections, each answered concisely then expanded.

## 6.4 Keyword Strategy Engine

**Primary data source: DataForSEO** (Keywords Data API, SERP API, Labs API, Backlinks API, AI Optimization API). Pay-as-you-go, $50 minimum, no subscription required.

**Secondary: Google Search Console API + GA4 API** (free, first-party data).

**Premium add-on: Ahrefs** (for customers with existing Enterprise accounts, connected via their own API key).

Workflow: Business outcomes first > current state assessment > keyword discovery > intent mapping > opportunity scoring > topic clusters > content velocity calibration > content calendar.

# PART 8: AI WRITING SYSTEM & QUALITY

## 8.1 How AI Detection Works

Methods: Perplexity analysis, burstiness analysis, statistical fingerprinting, frequency ratios, stylometric analysis, deep learning classification. Blog posts are the hardest format to pass detection on.

## 8.2 The AI Writing Tells Database

**Overused Words:** Verbs (delve, navigate, leverage, harness, illuminate, etc.), Adjectives (robust, comprehensive, seamless, pivotal, transformative, etc.), Nouns (landscape, realm, paradigm, synergy, etc.), Transitions (furthermore, moreover, it's worth noting, etc.)

**Structural Tells:** Rule of three, "Whether you're a [X], [Y], or [Z]", "In today's [X] landscape", problem-solution default, "In summary/conclusion" endings, excessive parallelism.

**Punctuation Tells:** Em dash overuse, excessive semicolons, consistent Oxford comma, curly quote artifacts.

**Tone Tells:** Flattery/superlatives, hedge word overuse, impersonal voice, synonym cycling, unnaturally consistent tone.

**Content Tells:** Vague instead of specific, no original insights, no personal experience, uniform heading capitalization.

## 8.3 Anti-Detection Writing System

Principle 1: Write like a specific human (voice model). Principle 2: Inject burstiness (variable sentence/paragraph lengths). Principle 3: Raise perplexity (non-obvious word choices). Principle 4: Structural unpredictability. Principle 5: Unique value injection. Principle 6: Narrative engine (hooks, momentum, payoff).

## 8.4 Detection Self-Testing Pipeline

Internal perplexity/burstiness scoring (code, no AI), banned word/phrase check (code), sentence length variance verification (code), section-level flagging, auto-revision of flagged sections (Sonnet), AI Detection Risk score shown to user.

## 8.5 AI Confidence & Training System

Writing sample scoring during onboarding, per-blog paragraph-level feedback, learning from human edits, confidence dashboard with trend line, A/B testing mode.

# PART 11: FAILURE MODES & GRACEFUL DEGRADATION

## 11.1 Philosophy

Every failure mode must have a defined recovery path. The system should never dead-end. It should degrade gracefully and tell the user exactly what's happening and what they can do.

## 11.2 Failure Mode Catalog

### FM-1: Blog Fails Quality Gates

**Trigger:** One or more of the 6 gates returns red.

**Response by gate:**
- Gate 1 (Strategic Alignment): "This keyword may cannibalize your existing post [Title]. Options: (a) Merge with existing post (refresh), (b) Differentiate angle (system suggests alternative angle), (c) Override and proceed."
- Gate 2 (SEO Score below 80): System auto-revises the specific failing elements (e.g., missing keyword in title, meta too long). Shows before/after. If still failing after auto-revision, flags specific items for user to fix manually.
- Gate 3 (AEO Score low): System adds structured Q&A section, injects statistics from knowledge base or suggests user provide data. If no data available: "This post would rank better with 2-3 statistics. Can you provide any data points? [Input field] Or we can publish without and add later."
- Gate 4 (Unique Value low): "This post doesn't yet contain a unique angle not found in the top 5 results. Options: (a) Add a customer story or data point [guided input], (b) Add your team's perspective on this topic [guided input], (c) Publish as-is (not recommended, system explains why)."
- Gate 5 (Detection Risk high): System auto-revises flagged sections. If still high after revision: specific paragraphs highlighted with "This section reads as AI-generated because [reason]. Suggested rewrite: [alternative]." User can accept, edit, or override.
- Gate 6 (Readability off-target): Auto-adjust sentence complexity. Show before/after reading level.

**Principle:** Auto-fix what can be auto-fixed. For what can't, give the user a clear explanation and specific actions. Never just say "failed" without a path forward.

### FM-2: Knowledge Base Too Thin

**Trigger:** User has provided minimal or no brand intelligence beyond the initial URL crawl.

**Response (tiered):**
- **Tier 1 (URL only, no additional input):** System writes using crawled data only. Voice model is approximate. Output quality labeled "Good" not "Great." After generation, prompt: "This blog is based only on your website. Adding [X] would make the next one significantly better." where X is the single highest-impact addition (e.g., "a paragraph about what makes your approach different" or "2-3 customer results").
- **Tier 2 (Some input, gaps remain):** System fills gaps with industry-standard content but flags the generic sections. "These 2 paragraphs are general industry knowledge. They'd be stronger with your company's specific experience. Want to add details now? [Inline edit fields expanding those paragraphs]."
- **Tier 3 (Rich input):** Full quality. No degradation.

**Principle:** Never refuse to generate because of thin input. Generate the best possible output with what's available, then show the user exactly how more input would improve it. Make input feel like an upgrade, not a prerequisite.

### FM-3: Keyword Has No Viable Angle

**Trigger:** Target keyword is either (a) impossibly competitive (KD 90+ with DR mismatch), (b) no search volume, (c) already perfectly covered by existing content, or (d) too ambiguous to determine intent.

**Response by cause:**
- (a) Too competitive: "This keyword has a difficulty of [X]. Based on your site's authority, ranking for this would require significant backlink investment and 6-12+ months. Alternative approaches: (1) Target a long-tail variant: [suggestions with lower KD], (2) Write this as a link-building piece (data study or thought leadership) to build authority, then revisit the head term later, (3) Proceed anyway (system notes expected timeline)."
- (b) No volume: "This keyword has very low search volume (<10/mo). Options: (1) Write it anyway for AEO citation potential (AI tools may still reference it), (2) Target a related keyword with volume: [suggestions], (3) Write as a thought leadership piece that earns links rather than search traffic."
- (c) Already covered: "You already have a post on this topic: [Title, link]. Options: (1) Refresh the existing post instead (recommended if it once ranked), (2) Write a complementary post from a different angle: [suggestions], (3) Write a more specific sub-topic: [suggestions]."
- (d) Ambiguous intent: "This keyword has mixed search intent. Google shows [informational, commercial, and navigational results]. Options: (1) Write for [most common intent] which is [X], (2) Write for [specific intent you choose], (3) Let us suggest a more specific keyword: [suggestions]."

### FM-4: Website Crawl Fails or Returns Insufficient Data

**Trigger:** URL is invalid, site blocks crawling, site has no parseable content, or site is a single-page app with no server-rendered content.

**Response:**
- Invalid URL: "We couldn't reach this URL. Check for typos or try again."
- Blocked: "Your site blocks our crawler. You can: (a) Whitelist our user agent [instructions], (b) Skip the crawl and tell us about your company instead [launches voice builder questionnaire]."
- Insufficient content: "Your site has very little text content for us to analyze. Let's build your voice profile from scratch instead. [Launches voice builder]."
- SPA: "Your site uses dynamic rendering that we can't fully crawl. You can: (a) Provide your sitemap URL, (b) Paste sample content directly, (c) Build your voice profile manually."

### FM-5: API Failures (DataForSEO, WordPress, GA4, GSC)

**Trigger:** External API returns error or timeout.

**Response:**
- DataForSEO down: Degrade gracefully. Skip keyword difficulty and volume data. Still generate the blog using the keyword the user provided. Flag: "We couldn't pull keyword data right now. Your blog was optimized for SEO best practices, but we'll add keyword metrics when the data source is back online."
- WordPress publishing fails: Save the content locally. "Publishing failed. Your blog is saved and ready. (a) Try again, (b) Download and publish manually, (c) Copy HTML to clipboard."
- GA4/GSC unavailable: Skip performance data. Show what's available. "Performance tracking is temporarily unavailable. Your blog is still published and being indexed."

**Principle:** No external dependency should prevent the core function (writing a blog) from working.

### FM-6: User Provides Contradictory Instructions

**Trigger:** User's voice profile says "casual and conversational" but they're requesting a formal whitepaper-style post. Or strategy says target one audience but the brief targets another.

**Response:** Surface the conflict, don't silently pick one. "Your voice profile says 'casual and conversational' but this brief targets enterprise CFOs. Options: (a) Write in your standard voice (casual), (b) Adjust tone for this audience (more formal, data-heavy) while keeping your core voice, (c) Create a separate voice variant for enterprise content."

### FM-7: Content Refresh Fails to Improve

**Trigger:** A refreshed post still doesn't rank after 4-8 weeks.

**Response:** Escalate diagnosis. "Your refreshed post [Title] hasn't improved after [X weeks]. Deeper analysis: (a) The competing content has [X] backlinks; you need link building, (b) The search intent may have shifted; here's what's ranking now: [analysis], (c) This keyword may no longer be viable for your domain authority. Recommended pivot: [alternative keyword]."

# PART 12: PROMPT ENGINEERING ARCHITECTURE

## 12.1 The Challenge

The prompts are the product. How we structure system prompts, inject voice profiles, select knowledge base context, and manage context windows determines writing quality more than any other engineering decision.

## 12.2 System Prompt Architecture

Every LLM call uses a layered prompt structure:

```
[LAYER 1: Base System Prompt]
  Role definition, writing principles, anti-detection rules, banned words/phrases.
  ~1,500 tokens. Cached across ALL calls for ALL workspaces.

[LAYER 2: Workspace Voice Profile]
  Brand voice attributes, tone guidelines, vocabulary preferences,
  never-say list, style rules, writing examples (best 2-3 paragraphs from approved content).
  ~800-1,500 tokens. Cached per workspace.

[LAYER 3: Task-Specific Instructions]
  Varies by task (writing, briefing, analysis, meta generation).
  Includes archetype-specific writing guidance.
  ~500-1,000 tokens. Cached per archetype.

[LAYER 4: Dynamic Context]
  Knowledge base excerpts (selected per topic), competitor analysis,
  keyword data, brief details.
  ~1,000-3,000 tokens. NOT cached (unique per blog).

[LAYER 5: User Message]
  The specific request: "Write a blog based on this brief: [brief]"
  ~200-500 tokens.
```

**Total input per writing call: ~4,000-6,500 tokens**
**Cached portion: ~2,800-4,000 tokens (60-65% of input)**
**Cache savings: 90% on cached tokens = significant cost reduction**

## 12.3 Knowledge Base Context Selection

The full knowledge base may be 50,000+ tokens. We can only inject 1,000-3,000 tokens per call. Selection strategy:

1. **Topic relevance scoring:** Embed all knowledge base entries. When writing a blog about topic X, retrieve the 5-10 most semantically similar entries.
2. **Entry type prioritization:** For each archetype, different entry types matter more:
   - Thought Leadership: prioritize company positions, hot takes, expert insights
   - How-To: prioritize process details, methodology, lessons learned
   - Data Study: prioritize proprietary data, benchmarks
   - Case Study: prioritize customer stories
   - Comparison: prioritize product knowledge, competitive positioning
3. **Recency weighting:** More recent entries weighted higher (fresher insights).
4. **Compression:** Summarize lengthy entries before injection. Use Haiku to compress a 500-word customer story into 100 words of key details.

## 12.5 Context Window Management

**Sonnet 4.6 context: 200K tokens standard (1M beta)**

For blog writing, we use ~4,000-6,500 input tokens. Well within limits. No truncation needed for standard blogs.

**When context gets large:**
- Definitive Guides (5,000+ words) may need longer context for coherence. Still under 15K tokens total.
- Knowledge base with many relevant entries: hard-cap at 3,000 tokens, use the selection strategy above.
- Competitor analysis: summarize to 500 tokens max. Full competitor content never enters the prompt.

**Rule: If a prompt approaches 10K input tokens, something is wrong. Compress or restructure.**

## 12.6 Prompt Versioning

All prompts are versioned and tracked. When a prompt change is made:
1. A/B test against 10 blog generations before rolling out
2. Measure: voice consistency score, SEO score, detection risk score, user feedback
3. Only promote if all metrics are equal or better
4. Keep rollback capability for 30 days

# APPENDIX A: WORD & PHRASE BLACKLIST

**Banned Verbs:** delve, leverage (as verb), navigate (metaphorical), harness, illuminate, underscore, spearhead, streamline, revolutionize, catalyze, embark, foster, cultivate, bolster, amplify

**Banned Adjectives:** robust, comprehensive (filler), seamless, pivotal, transformative, groundbreaking, cutting-edge, holistic, nuanced (overused), multifaceted, game-changing, unparalleled, bespoke, meticulous (overused), paramount

**Banned Nouns:** landscape (metaphorical), realm, paradigm, synergy, ecosystem (metaphorical), tapestry, nexus, interplay

**Banned Phrases:** "In today's [X]," "Whether you're a [X], [Y], or [Z]," "At its core," "It's worth noting," "It is important to note," "At the end of the day," "In summary," "In conclusion," "Let's dive in," "Let's delve into," "Not only...but also" (as pattern), "In the realm of," "When it comes to," "The ever-evolving," "Moving forward," "That said" (overuse), "It goes without saying," "Needless to say," "As we all know," "Have you ever wondered"

**Banned Punctuation:** Em dashes, excessive semicolons, mechanical bolding

**Banned Structures:** Always-three lists, default problem-solution, summary conclusions, "Despite" endings, excessive parallelism

# APPENDIX D: ANTI-DETECTION BENCHMARKS

**Burstiness:** Sentence length SD: 8+ words. 15%+ under 8 words. 10%+ over 25 words. Max 3 consecutive within 5 words of each other.

**Perplexity:** Average word predictability: 60-75%. 20%+ non-obvious choices.

**Structure:** Max 2 consecutive same-length paragraphs. 3+ lengths per 500 words. Varied list lengths. Varied heading styles.

**Voice:** 70%+ vocabulary overlap with voice model. Zero banned words. 1+ brand expression per 500 words.
